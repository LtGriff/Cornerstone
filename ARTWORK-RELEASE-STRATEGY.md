# Cornerstone artwork rights and release strategy

Reviewed 2026-10-23 against local commit `fade1bdfa3ec3c2d630aa7701c6ebea9fe1c8391` and the linked provider documents. This is a product and technical risk assessment, **not legal advice or permission**. Terms can change; obtain qualified IP/platform counsel and written provider/rightsholder decisions before treating an uncertain use as cleared.

## Executive summary

Cornerstone's distinctive product is four meaningful music cornerstones shaping a customizable, animated 60-tile wallpaper. That concept can remain. The current *album-art export pipeline*, however, is **not cleared for general public distribution**: the app mixes Spotify, Cover Art Archive, Apple-hosted and uploaded images into downloadable PNGs, while none of the reviewed sources supplies a blanket license for that use. Spotify's identified terms specifically restrict artwork alteration and downloading. A free product, personal use, or five-person beta does not itself remove copyright or API-contract obligations.

Recommended path: continue technical testing privately without claiming rights clearance; design a source-aware artwork policy and a rights-cleared visual/export lane; then obtain provider/rightsholder guidance before enabling public album-art downloads. Keep Spotify for personalized discovery only to the extent its current developer agreement and the approved-account test permit. **Live Spotify sign-in is still NOT VERIFIED** (see [BETA-VALIDATION.md](BETA-VALIDATION.md)). No app behavior was changed in Phase 7C.

Current project JSON can contain provider artwork URLs and embedded uploaded data URLs. The code does not maintain per-image rights evidence, attribution requirements, expiry, or export eligibility.

The ratings apply to the *present Cornerstone implementation*, not to every possible app. Copyright and API contracts are separate layers; satisfying one does not satisfy the other.

The [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html) explains that changing a work does not give the changer ownership of the underlying work. Whether a particular personal wallpaper is fair use depends on facts and jurisdiction; neither automation nor this document can decide that. Get counsel for derivative/compilation, fair-use, territorial and consumer-facing risk questions.

## Release models

| Model | What remains / technical changes | Product compromise, complexity and cost | Downloadable wallpapers? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Licensed album-art experience** | Keep recognizable covers, rack, animated grid, four corners, arrangements, color controls, and both exports. Obtain written grants from the appropriate cover-art owners **and** comply with each API contract. Record permitted cover IDs, crops, animation, composites, exports, territories, terms, attribution and commercial scope; gate each use accordingly. | Highest fidelity to the original vision, but many rightsholders/releases, negotiations, legal review, renewals and likely licensing fees. Spotify's platform rules may still disallow an export of Spotify-supplied pixels even if a separate copyright license exists; obtain platform permission or use an independently licensed delivery source in compliance with all contracts. | **Yes, only for covers and uses specifically cleared in writing**; otherwise disable those covers' export, not the entire product by assumption. |
| **B. Maintain source/provenance separation; do not copy Spotify cover URLs into an export-eligible asset. Four cornerstone *album identities* can drive matching imagery and color choices when permitted by provider terms. | Some walls will not show recognizable album covers. Requires a curated/licensed art catalog, mapping rules, ongoing asset acquisition and review; moderate-to-high build effort and potentially creator/license costs. | **C. Original art, color fields and properly licensed user assets** | Use the existing 60-tile mechanics, fixed four corners, gradients, arrangements, manual replacement and exports with original graphics/colors. Optionally include user-provided art under a rights-aware local workflow. Album names can still personalize the experience only when metadata use is lawful under the chosen source. | Lowest dependency on cover-art clearance, but less instantly recognizable as an album wall. Do not implement model B or C by silently swapping providers or removing the current creative interactions. Each should be a deliberate, labeled product lane. All three can retain frosted styling, personalized four-color gradients, vinyl-rack progress, replacement, manual arranging, and phone/desktop exports; imagery eligibility differs.

## Technical separation to plan (not implemented)

1. Create explicit provenance on every album/asset: metadata provider, image provider, original URL/asset ID, copyright/licensor evidence, terms version, attribution/link requirement, permitted display/transform/export flags, expiry and review date. Do **not** infer rights from hostname, CORS, successful fetch, or user upload.
2. Split *music identity/listening data* from *renderable artwork*. A Spotify album ID and a CAA release ID can identify music without treating either provider's thumbnail as an export asset. Keep the four physical corner assignments and saved-project compatibility intact.
3. Centralize eligibility checks at display, animation, color analysis, caching, sample screenshot bundling, project backup, and PNG export. An export should fail with a clear choice of rights-cleared replacement or original/color lane; a successful canvas call must not override the policy.
4. Make caching/deletion source-aware. Define retention, expiry, refresh, revocation and deletion behavior for Spotify-derived metadata, artwork URLs, colors and project backups; review with Spotify before implementing assumptions about permissible storage.
5. Establish an asset/license register and a test manifest for demo screenshots and defaults. Add an automated check that prevents unreviewed images from entering a public release package or export path. This is a proposed future safeguard, not a current guarantee.
6. Keep provider attribution and album links next to eligible catalog displays. A downloaded PNG cannot carry a clickable per-album link in the same way; resolve that conflict with provider guidance rather than adding tiny text and assuming compliance.

## Unresolved questions requiring answers

- **Spotify:** Will Spotify permit this non-streaming discovery use, the current four-color analysis outside playback, client-side metadata/project retention, tile flip/frost presentation, or any cover-wall export? Which exact current branding/link-back requirements apply? Will its API access mode cover the planned audience? Obtain written platform guidance; also address underlying cover copyrights. See [terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms), [policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy), [design](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design).
- **Apple:** What exact chart feed and agreement produced `dist/top-songs.js`? Can those images appear in an aesthetic wall, packaged example, or downloadable PNG under that agreement and the underlying owners' rights? The Apple docs above are a warning and a starting point, not proof of the static list's precise license.
- **CAA/labels/artists:** Who can grant the necessary rights per cover, including edits, compilation and end-user downloads? Does any contemplated license cover the actual cropped, animated and exported treatment, territory and duration? CAA availability is not that grant.
- **Tre's Picks:** Can every cover in the two bundled screenshots be cleared, or should the public demo be rebuilt with independently cleared imagery?
- **User content and legal scope:** What upload representations, permissions, takedown process, privacy/retention disclosures, age/territory limits and fair-use assessment are appropriate? Qualified IP counsel should review before a public art-export launch. Do not treat a checkbox as proof of ownership.
- **Real Spotify validation:** The approved-user PKCE flow, consent, import, quota handling, cancellation and reconnect need the sanitized live test in [BETA-VALIDATION.md](BETA-VALIDATION.md); mocks and policy reading do not complete it.

## Recommended staged release decision

1. **Local personal testing:** Continue technical and UX work, clearly label rights status unresolved. Do not describe local use as categorically lawful. Keep personal project files and Spotify credentials out of shared reports.
2. **Controlled beta:** Test with approved accounts only after reviewing the beta's actual display/export behavior with counsel/provider guidance. A private or free beta is **not** a copyright exemption. If permission is absent, use a rights-cleared demo/visual lane and consider a proposed feature flag that blocks unlicensed album-art export. Do not implement the flag as an implicit decision in this phase.
3. **Public website:** Publish only after the default wall, Tre's Picks assets, provider attribution, catalog use, persistence and user notices have an approved basis. A public site without download still distributes bundled examples and displays third-party art; its scope also needs review.
4. **Public downloadable album-art wallpapers:** Treat as a separate, higher bar. Require a documented license and platform compatibility for the exact final composition, alterations, end-user file delivery, and commercial plan. Until then, public PNG export should be confined to original/independently licensed imagery or another counsel-approved lane.

### Prioritized next steps

**Blockers before broad public art release:** identify the chart-feed contract; obtain IP counsel review of current art sources/exports and Tre's screenshots; seek Spotify platform guidance; secure artwork rights or choose a rights-cleared export lane. **Blocker before claiming Spotify works live:** complete an approved-account test without logging private data. **Important:** inventory existing artwork and build provenance/eligibility architecture; validate MusicBrainz identification and provider reliability separately. **Later:** negotiate expanded art licenses if recognizability is commercially central, and evaluate public sharing/monetization only against the final grants and policies.

**Decision today:** Cornerstone is technically testable, but the reviewed evidence does **not** clear its present album-cover wall and PNG exports for unrestricted public release. Preserve the creative product and resolve the imagery lane before making that promise.
